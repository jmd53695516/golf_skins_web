# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is the group's organizer — the "banker" who runs the money games for a golf outing. After the round they collect everyone's paper scorecards, photograph them, and are on the hook for settling up who owes what. They are almost always standing in the parking lot or clubhouse with a phone in one hand, often 50 or older, wanting the math done before people scatter. The rest of the group are downstream beneficiaries who just want to trust the final number; they don't operate the app.

## Product Purpose

The app turns a photo of a paper scorecard into settled results for the side-games a group plays for money. Claude reads the scorecard, the organizer reviews and corrects the extracted data, and the app calculates the outcome. It exists so one person can settle bets on the spot without doing error-prone math by hand. Success is that the group trusts the numbers, nobody re-litigates who owes what, and even the tricky formats come out right.

## Positioning

The one tool that scores real-money golf side-games — including the formats that are genuinely hard to do by hand (Nassau with stacked presses, Vegas birdie flips, Quota, 2 Net Better Ball, Skins) — straight from a photo of the scorecard, so the banker settles up before anyone leaves the lot.

## Brand Personality

Sleek and modern with quiet confidence. This should feel like a premium sports or finance app: fast, precise, data-forward, and out of the way. The voice is crisp and plain-spoken with a little sporting edge — it states results as facts, not decorations. The organizer should feel like they're holding the official ruling, not a novelty toy.

## Anti-references

Not clip-art or cartoonish — no cheesy golf mascots, novelty fonts, or "free scorecard app" energy. Not a generic SaaS dashboard — no cold blue-and-gray, could-be-any-B2B blandness. Not a cluttered spreadsheet — no wall of tiny numbers and gridlines that fall apart on a phone. The current Augusta/Masters ornamental treatment (deep-green + gold + serif) is explicitly open to being rethought toward the sleek-modern direction above.

## Design Principles

The ruling is final. Results are the product; they must look trustworthy and be correct. Presentation should make the outcome feel authoritative and unambiguous.

Built for the parking lot. Phone-first, one-handed, glance-and-go. Primary actions sit within thumb reach; nothing assumes a desk or a mouse.

Handle complexity, don't expose it. The hard games get scored correctly, but the interface stays simple — the player sees a clean answer, not the machinery that produced it.

Premium without the costume. Earn trust the way a good sports or finance app does — through clarity, speed, and restraint — not by dressing up as a country club.

Legible for the whole group. Generous type sizes and large tap targets so a 50-something reading it at arm's length has no trouble.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Designed for real one-handed phone use with key actions reachable by thumb, and tuned for older eyes with generous text sizes and large tap targets. Prioritize strong text/background contrast and clear legibility given the device-in-hand, outdoor context.
